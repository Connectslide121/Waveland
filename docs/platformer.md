# The platformer's level

`src/games/platformer/level` builds the platformer's level in world space from the core's `LevelMap`, and proves it can be finished.

## The design

A song-locked auto-runner, like Bit.Trip Runner or Geometry Dash: the player never walks. Their x comes only from song time, `xAtTime(level, t)` (in `level.ts`, behind `LevelWorld.xAt` for the physics), so they are always at the anchor (`ANCHOR_X` into the view) and always in time with the music: everything is placed with the same function, so they reach each thing on its beat. The camera scrolls with song time at a speed from the tempo (`scrollSpeed` in `physics.ts`: the tempo folded into 80–160 BPM, 110 px per folded beat, so 147–293 px/s). `xAtTime` is a function of the level so a later tempo map only changes it, not its callers.

Depth comes from abilities, not walking:

- **Jump**: variable height (letting go cuts the rise), with coyote time and a jump buffer.
- **Dash**: holds the cube level (no gravity) for `DASH_TIME` (0.25 s, half a beat at 120 BPM), once per time in the air, refilled on the ground once it ends; it works on the ground too. It smashes through dash gates, and after a jump it clears pits a jump alone can't.
- **Slam**: from the air, a fast drop (`SLAM_SPEED`) to whatever is below, to get under an overhang in time. A slam landing on the beat scores as an accent.

Running into the side of a column, a hurdle or an overhang is a death ("wall"): there is no stopping. So is falling into a pit, touching spikes that are out, and passing a dash gate without dashing. Surviving until the song ends finishes the level.

| Element | From the music | In the level |
|---|---|---|
| Equaliser columns | Bass energy per beat | Columns, one per beat, whose height eases to the bass level on every beat; they carry the player, and one that has risen above the feet is a wall to time a jump over |
| Bridges | Snares | Slabs over a pit that come out on a snare and stay a beat or two, then are gone for at least a beat (`BRIDGE_GAP`) before the next snare brings them back, so a backbeat never keeps them out for good |
| Spikes | Off-beats | Strips that are out from each off-beat to the next beat; cross on the beat or jump |
| Gaps | Beats | Pits to jump, taking off on a beat, landing higher or lower; sized by the air time they need (a share of a full jump's, `JUMP_AIR`) |
| Dash gaps | Beats | Pits too wide for a jump: their air time is between a jump's reach (`JUMP_REACH`, with coyote time) and a jump and a dash at its top, so they take both |
| Dash gates | Snares, else strong kicks, else beats | Orange barriers from the ground into the sky. The player meets one `GATE_GRACE` (0.1 s) after its beat, so a dash pressed on the beat, or a little either side, gets through |
| Overhangs | Beats | A thin low hurdle to jump on a beat, then an overhang hanging from the sky that comes before a full jump has come down: slam after the hurdle (or hop short) to get under it |
| Coins | Hi-hats | Where the anchored player is on the hat, over the ground or up in the jump over a pit; where there are no hats for a while (a quiet intro), on every other beat instead. Never closer than 26 px to another coin or a gem, so fast hats don't pile up |
| Set piece | Drops | Two bars (four on fast songs): a staircase of bars to climb on the beat, a plateau, gems on every step |
| Calm stretches | Intros, breakdowns, outros | Plain wide ground, now and then a low step or an easy gap |

The obstacle builders take their moments from outside (`pitAt(x, takeoff, air)`, `gateAt(x, time, top)`, `overhangAt(x, from, to, h, clearance)`), so the music can size them. Which obstacle a bar gets is still chosen per section (`PLANS`), weighted at random from the section's kinds.

**Teaching.** Dash and slam are taught before anything else needs them: the first easy spot (a calm section, or a bar that would be plain ground) gets a dash gate, the next one an overhang, built easier. Obstacles that need an ability (`CHUNK_NEEDS`) only come after its lesson. The level keeps the lessons (`PlatformerLevel.lessons`), and the HUD shows a one-line hint with the current bindings ("Right click / Shift: dash") from a bar before the lesson until the player is past it, once per session.

Fast songs (bars under 340 px) are laid out two bars per stretch, drops four, and on every other beat when beats are under 70 px: two such bars are too short for a staircase.

## Difficulty

`DIFFICULTY` in `generate.ts`: easy, normal and hard change how often a bar in a busy section gets an obstacle, gap air times (a share of a full jump's), where dash gaps sit between a jump's reach and a dash's, how many gates a stretch gets, how long overhangs are and how much room they leave, height changes, spike lengths, how long bridges stay, how far equalisers move, the staircase's step height and how many hats get a coin.

## Always completable

`physics.ts` has the player's constants (jump velocity, gravity, coyote time, dash time, slam speed, box size) and `stepPlayer`, the one step function the game and the validator both run, at fixed `PHYSICS_DT` steps, against the level's moving parts at song time (`LevelWorld`). Its input is `{ jump, release, dash, slam }`; x is set from `world.xAt(t)` every step, never from input.

The validator (`validate.ts`) is a search over the player's inputs. With x fixed by time, every candidate at a moment is at the same x, so a candidate is only its height, speed, ground, dash charge and timer and slam. Every three steps each candidate tries doing nothing, jumping, letting go of jump (a lower jump, so the game's variable height is in the search), dashing and slamming, whichever can change something. Candidates in the same state are merged, and the rest thinned evenly by height within each kind (on the ground, dash ready, dashing, dash spent, slamming), so those with a dash still to use aren't crowded out. Feet more than a step below the lowest surface (`LOWEST_TOP`) can never land again and are dropped. It can only miss ways through, never invent one, so a level it passes can really be finished. `validateLevel(level, without)` can leave dash or slam out, so tests prove a lesson needs its ability.

The generator builds a bar (or two) at a time and runs the search through it before going on. Each stretch's search stops on a decision step while the player's box is still wholly inside the stretch, so every candidate has only met what is built, just as in the finished level, and the incremental proof agrees with `validateLevel`. A stretch nobody gets through, or that leaves nobody on the ground, is built again easier: narrower gaps and lower steps, then longer bridges and shorter spikes, then a ledge in each gap, and at last plain ground at the height the last stretch ended, which is always passable. `Chunk.repairs` counts the tries; the song details view shows them as "eased". A three-minute song builds in about a second.

Deterministic: the generator's randomness is seeded by the song's map seed, the difficulty and the bar, so the same song at the same difficulty always gives the same level, and a repair never changes the bars after it.

## Playing it

`src/games/platformer/game.ts` starts a session: it builds the level in the worker (at normal difficulty, until there is a picker), decodes the song and runs Phaser.

- **Song time only.** Every frame the session reads `AudioClock.time` and `PlatformerRun.update` (`run.ts`) runs the fixed `PHYSICS_DT` steps of `stepPlayer` up to it, so the game moves exactly as the validator checked. The scene (`scene.ts`) draws the level as it is at that time: equaliser heights, bridges, spikes, gates, the camera at `cameraAt(level, t)` (so the cube stays at the anchor), the squash, the effects. Nothing moves by frame time.
- **Feel.** Coyote time is in the physics. The jump buffer (`JUMP_BUFFER`) is in the run: a jump or dash press counts for that long, so one pressed just before landing happens on landing. A slam press only counts in its own frame (held over, it would cut the next jump short). Variable height is `release` in `stepPlayer`: letting go of jump while rising cuts the speed to `JUMP_CUT_VELOCITY`; the validator lets go too, so short hops are part of what it proves.
- **Controls** (`controls.ts`, from the bindings in Settings): by default Space, W or ↑ to jump; Shift, J, X or right click to dash; S, ↓ or left click to slam; Esc to pause. Gamepad: A to jump, X or RB to dash, B or down (d-pad or left stick) to slam, Start to pause. Mouse buttons play on the game's surface (`mousedown` per button, so both at once do both), never on HUD or menu buttons, and the browser's context menu is kept shut there. Every device's presses go the same way into the run's fixed steps. The pause menu lists the current bindings.
- **Deaths.** The run goes back to its checkpoint: the start of the stretch the current song section begins in (stretches start on a bar line on plain ground). The death effect plays for `DEATH_TIME` of song time, then the music rewinds to one bar before the checkpoint and the player waits through that bar as a 4-3-2-1 count-in. At the start the count-in is a bar of silence: `AudioClock.play` from a negative time.
- **Score.** Coins and gems, and actions within `PERFECT_WINDOW`/`GOOD_WINDOW` of a beat (jump takeoffs, jump landings, dashes and slam landings; a slam landing on the beat is an accent, worth `ACCENT_FACTOR` times as much), add to a combo that raises the multiplier every `COMBO_STEP` up to ×4. An action off the beat or a death breaks the combo, and a death puts the score back to what it was at the checkpoint. Accuracy on the results screen is the share of those actions on the beat.
- **Look** (`scene.ts`, Neon arcade after `docs/concept/look.html`): bloom on the camera (Phaser's `AddEffectBloom`) over the synth grid rolling one row per beat, faint wave lines and the violet speaker sending out a ring on every kick; equalisers and ground with bright caps, dotted cyan bridges (a faint dotted outline where a bridge will be, blinking in over the half beat before its snare), orange spikes that rise a little just before they come out; orange dash gates humming on the beat, whose bars fly on ahead when dashed through; overhangs in the world's violet hanging from the sky, with an orange face where they would hit. The cube flips once per jump (from `run.jumpedAt`), squashes and stretches and leaves a light trail; a dash stretches it long and flat with cyan afterimages and speed lines streaming back, so it reads as a burst forward though the cube stays at the anchor; a slam stretches it tall with a streak above, and its landing sends a shockwave along the ground, magenta on the beat and cyan off it. Landings raise dust, pickups spark, deaths shatter the cube and shake the screen. Every effect is drawn from song time, so a pause freezes it and a rewind plays it again.
- **The drop's set piece**: through each drop chunk the sky flashes magenta and violet on every beat, two big waveforms ripple, particles burst from the player on every beat over the magenta staircase and DROP thumps at the top; then it calms down over two bars.
- **Effects intensity** (Settings): reduced turns off the flashes (the drop glows steadily instead) and the screen shake; it follows `prefers-reduced-motion` until the player picks.
- **Frame budget** (`scene.ts`, `perf.ts`): ground, steps and ledges never move, so they are drawn once into 512 px tile textures as they scroll in (`#bakeTile`); bridges are pooled images of one dot texture; equalisers, spikes and pickups are culled to the view by binary search; the frame loop allocates nothing (the trail is a ring of typed arrays, floating texts and the frame state are reused). The bloom's threshold pass writes to a half-size target, so the blur works on a quarter of the pixels and the blend scales it back up. `FrameMonitor` times every frame (from Phaser's `PRE_STEP` to `POST_RENDER`): when most frames of a second are over 19 ms apart, the quality steps down (`QUALITY`: fewer blur passes, then a quarter-size bloom, fewer particles, fewer wave points), and Settings says "Lowered automatically to keep the game smooth" next to Effects while the game runs. Measured at 1920×1080 on a 150 BPM rock test song (desktop, RTX 5070 Ti, Chromium; frames stepped at 60 fps of song time): CPU per frame calm 3.4 → 1.7 ms, busy stretches with bridges 5.0 → 2.2 ms mean and 9.4 → 4.6 ms worst, drops 2.8 → 2.5 ms mean, worst under 5 ms; GPU per frame 0.47 → 0.26 ms, the bloom's share 0.41 → about 0.18 ms. A laptop is several times slower on both, so the numbers are for comparing changes, not a promise.
- **Settings** (`src/shell/settings.ts`, from the title screen and the pause menu): music volume (a shared gain node, applied at once), effects intensity, the frame rate readout (Show puts FPS and the mean CPU ms per frame in the HUD's bottom right corner), and the audio latency offset, nudged in 5 ms steps or calibrated by tapping along to a 100 BPM metronome (the median lateness of the taps; uneven taps are turned down), and the controls (`src/shell/controls-settings.ts`): jump, dash and slam in aligned columns, three key slots, two mouse and two gamepad slots each. Click a slot and press the key or button (a mouse button anywhere, a newly pressed gamepad button); Escape cancels, Backspace clears. An input another action had moves over and the row says so ("Shift was dash; now slam"). Esc and Start always pause and can't be bound. Reset puts one action, or all, back to the defaults. The bindings are kept in the stored settings (`getBindings` in `src/core/settings.ts`).
- **HUD and menus** (`ui.ts`) are HTML over the canvas: song and BPM, beat dots, score and combo, the count-in, progress with the drops marked, the first-time hint for an ability; the pause menu (Esc: Resume, Restart, Settings, Quit, and the controls as bound) and the results screen (Retry, Back to menu). Switching away from the tab pauses. Resume holds the player where they stopped and rewinds the music `RESUME_BEATS` (two beats) for a short count-in, so play never starts again mid-jump without warning.

## Where to look

- Profiling: in dev (`npm run dev`) the running session is `window.waveland` (its `scene`, `run`, `clock`), for timing frames from the browser's console.
- Song details view, below the analysis: a side-on preview of the level at a picked difficulty, following the playhead (`src/shell/level-preview.ts`).
- `generateLevelInWorker(map, difficulty)` builds it off the main thread; a three-minute song takes about a second.
