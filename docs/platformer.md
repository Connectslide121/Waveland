# The platformer's level

`src/games/platformer/level` builds the platformer's level in world space from the core's `LevelMap`, and proves it can be finished.

## The design

A song-locked auto-runner, like Bit.Trip Runner or Geometry Dash: the player never walks. Their x comes only from song time, `xAtTime(level, t)` (in `level.ts`, behind `LevelWorld.xAt` for the physics), so they are always at the anchor and always in time with the music: everything is placed with the same function, so they reach each thing on its beat. The camera scrolls with song time at a speed from the tempo, following the song's tempo map (`docs/core.md`): each tempo segment at `scrollSpeed` of its tempo (`physics.ts`: the tempo folded into 80–160 BPM, 110 px per folded beat, so 147–293 px/s), easing to the next segment's speed over that segment's first beat, so the player speeds up and slows down with the song without a jerk. A switch to half or double time folds to the same speed and keeps it. The level keeps this as `scroll`, pieces of steady speed and of easing (`scrollFor`); `xAtTime` is their integral, `speedAt` the speed and `timeAtX` the inverse. The anchor is `ANCHOR_SHARE` (40%) of the screen's width, whatever its shape: the scene fits the 960 × 540 view's height to the screen (its width on screens narrower than 16:9), so a wider screen shows more level either side (`ANCHOR_X`, 384, is the anchor in the 16:9 view). The level is built for the widest screen, 32:9 (`MAX_VIEW_WIDTH`, `VIEW_AHEAD` and `VIEW_BEHIND` in `physics.ts`): ground reshaping into a new style, columns and slabs starting to move, the run-in ground under the count-in and the run-out past the end all happen out of its sight, so an ultrawide screen never sees them change. The level also keeps the song's beat grid (`grid`), which spikes, scoring, the HUD and the scene read through the core's tempo-map helpers.

Depth comes from abilities, not walking:

- **Jump**: variable height (letting go cuts the rise), with coyote time and a jump buffer.
- **Dash**: holds the cube level (no gravity) for `DASH_TIME` (0.25 s, half a beat at 120 BPM), once per time in the air, refilled on the ground once it ends; it works on the ground too. It smashes through dash gates, and after a jump it clears pits a jump alone can't.
- **Slam**: from the air, a fast drop (`SLAM_SPEED`) to whatever is below. It breaks through slam plates, the only thing that does, to get under the low ceiling past them. A slam landing on a kick or a downbeat scores as an accent.

Running into the side of a column or an overhang is a death ("wall"): there is no stopping. So is falling into a pit, touching spikes that are out, and passing a dash gate without dashing. Surviving until the song ends finishes the level.

## The music-to-obstacle grammar

Every obstacle comes from a musical moment the core found (`LevelMap.features` and the events, `docs/core.md`), and the music sizes it, so someone who knows the song recognises its moments in the level. `momentsOf` in `generate.ts` turns each moment into a candidate obstacle with the beats it takes up; `planBars` picks each bar's; `compose` builds them on the ground one after another.

| Musical moment | Obstacle | Sized by |
|---|---|---|
| Held sound (a note, a chord, a ringing cymbal: `Sustain`) | A pit taking off on its hit: a jump, then a dash held over the note | Its length: the player is in the air as long as it holds and lands as it ends, up to what the difficulty lets a jump and a dash reach (`held`, between `JUMP_REACH` and a jump and dash's). Shorter than a jump's reach, it is a plain pit |
| Stop or break (`Stop`) | The floor drops out while the band does: a pit landing as the band comes back in | Its length: off on its start when a jump (and a dash) reaches that far, else on the latest sixteenth that does; wide stops need the dash |
| Kick (on a beat or an "and": one on a sixteenth is a chug or a pickup, `KICK_ON_GRID`) | A short gap, taking off on the kick: the ground ends under the player's middle on the kick, so a jump on it looks like a jump at the edge and one a little late (half the player, then coyote time) still makes it | Air a share of a full jump's (`gap`), a little more for a harder kick; it lands toward the height of the song's energy (`contourTop`), at most `rise` up or down |
| Snare | A pit with a bridge that comes out on the snare (`bridgeTimes`): jump on the beat before it (the eighth before on a slow song, when a jump wouldn't last a beat), the bridge lights up under you on the snare, land on it, and jump off on the first beat after landing over the rest of the pit (still ground after it, so a jump off on the beat always lands). Two presses on beats; staying on it or jumping off late drops you in. It used to be a pit exactly as long as the bridge was up, crossed without a press. | The gap after it (`BRIDGE_GAP_SHARE` of a kick's `gap`, at least `BRIDGE_MIN_AIR`) |
| Accent (several bands at once, louder than the beats around) | A dash gate. The player meets it `GATE_GRACE` (0.1 s) after its beat, so a dash pressed on the beat, or a little either side, gets through | — |
| Syncopation (an accented kick or snare off the beat, against the groove: `docs/core.md`) | Spikes a jump on the hit, or up to `PRESS_GRACE` (0.1 s) after it, clears (`spikesAt`; spikes are out from each off-beat to the next beat) | Strip length (`spike` beats) |
| Heavy kick (clearly stronger than every kick in the two bars before); on hard also the downbeat opening each 4-bar phrase of a chorus or a drop, unless a crash there makes it a gate | A slam plate: from `SLAM_APPROACH` before the kick the floor is a cracked plate over ground `SLAM_DROP` (56 px) lower, and a low ceiling over that ground starts where the plate ends, on the kick. Running on along the plate hits the ceiling's face; a jump and a slam anywhere on it break through, landing under the ceiling (on the kick when timed to it). The level goes on from the lower ground | How long the ceiling lasts (`overhang`), how much room it leaves (`clearance`) |
| Fill (a dense run of kicks, snares or toms into a bar line) | A raised run of steps going down one per hit; once slam is taught (on normal and hard: `slamFills`), its last two steps are slam plates over the ground with a low ceiling from the bar line, so the fill ends in a slam onto the bar line. Near the top of the view the ground steps down first, so the steps fit | Step height (half of `stepRise`), up to `RISER_MAX` in all |
| Hi-hats | Coins where the anchored player is on the hat, over the ground or up in the jump over a pit; on every other beat where there are no hats for a while. Never closer than 26 px to another coin or a gem | `coinEvery` |
| Bass under the kicks | Equaliser columns, one per beat, whose height eases to the bass level on every beat, taller on louder kicks; one risen above the feet is a wall to time a jump over | `eqRange`, `eqGap` |
| Drop | Three bars (four on fast songs): a staircase of up to seven steps to climb on the beat (each riser where a jump on its beat, or up to `PRESS_GRACE` after it, is already over it: `clearFrom`), a plateau with an arc of gems, then low hurdles on its beats (`hurdle` solids, `HURDLE_HEIGHT`, placed with `clearFrom` so a jump on the beat clears them, every other beat or as far apart as a jump needs), gems on every step | `stepRise` |
| Energy contour | The ground climbs where the song gets louder and comes down where it gets quieter (the mood's smoothed intensity): gaps land toward it, and plain stretches step toward it on a kick, by their terrain style (see [Terrain styles](#terrain-styles)) | `CONTOUR_RANGE` |

**How many.** The rare moments (stops, held sounds, fills, accents, heavy kicks) always become obstacles when they fit. How many common ones (kicks, snares, syncopations) a bar gets follows how busy the music is: its kicks and snares per beat and its energy, times the difficulty's `hazard`, the fractions carried from bar to bar. A quiet part is sparse because the music is: there are no dice. **A floor** under that: every busy bar asks for something (`busyFloor` a bar, `peakFloor` in a chorus or a drop, fractions carried from bar to bar: easy ½ and 1, normal 1 and 1½, hard 1 and 2). A bar short of it takes more of its own common moments, then a gap on its strongest free beat (the downbeat, then the third: reason "the beat"), kept when the bar repeats. Calm parts (intro, breakdown, outro, or a section that feels calm) have no floor. A playtest of a punk song found 38% of it in stretches of two bars or more with nothing to press; with the floor and real bridges, 13%, nearly all of it the intro, the breakdown and the outro. Strongest first (`SALIENCE`: stop, held, fill, accent, heavy kick, equaliser, syncopation, snare, kick), never overlapping; a rare moment pushes out a common one planned in the bar before. The only randomness left breaks the one tie the music leaves open: a bar of kicks alone over pumping bass gets a gap on its strongest kick or the equalisers, seeded by the bar.

**On the pulse.** A press the player can't count in is a reflex, not music. A recorded play of a dense rock song ("Save run data") had 81 of its 172 obstacles on sixteenths (spikes on off-beat hits, gates on accents), one in each bar at a spot nobody hears, and the presses came scattered up to 120 ms either side of the eighths: the player was reacting to what scrolled in. So on easy and normal every press is on a beat or an "and": an accent or an off-beat hit on a sixteenth gets no obstacle (`sixteenths`, hard only). Off-beat hits are moments only while they are rare: more than `SYNC_PER_BAR` (1.5) a bar over the `SYNC_WINDOW` (16) beats either side and they are the song's texture, and get no spikes at any difficulty. Among a bar's moments of one kind, the ones where the bar a phrase back had its obstacles go first, then the ones on the beat before the "ands", then the strongest: a part keeps its pattern from phrase to phrase even where the song isn't heard to repeat. On that song: 3 of 111 obstacles on a sixteenth on normal, none of them a press.

**Repeats.** When the song plays a bar again (`BarInfo.repeats`: a riff, a chorus), the level gives it the obstacles that bar had a phrase (`PHRASE_BARS`, 4 bars) back when the song played it there, else the first time it was played, each on its own moment there (one the repeat doesn't have is left out), so the player learns the pattern as they learn the song. A repeated obstacle says so (`Obstacle.repeats`, "as in bar 12"). A loop of a bar or two (a four-on-the-floor intro) would otherwise copy its first bar throughout, an obstacle every bar or none at all as the running count fell: its first phrase is planned afresh, bar by bar, and then repeats as a phrase. On hard, a bar played a third time or more gets one common obstacle of its own as well.

**Section mood.** Calm sections (intro, breakdown, outro) leave out the busy music's obstacles (spikes, bridges, equalisers), halve the common ones and make them easier (`CALM_EASE`); the rare moments keep their size. A build gets denser as it goes: up to `BUILD_DENSER` (80%) more common obstacles by its end than its music alone gives.

## Terrain styles

The obstacles stay on their moments; the ground around them changes with the song. Each stretch gets a terrain style from its section's feel (`LevelMap.moods`, `docs/core.md`; read half a beat into the stretch, as the analysis' section lines sit a hair off the grid's bar lines), recorded as `Chunk.style`. `terrain.ts` reshapes the plain ground between the obstacles; ground under spikes, gates and ceilings, hurdles and a fill's steps keep their height. Everything that moves is in the solids' keys (`Solid.keys`, eased over `Solid.ease`), so the physics, and so the validator, sees it exactly as it is drawn.

| Feel | Style | The ground |
|---|---|---|
| Calm, breakdown | `float` | Wide floating slabs (`float` solids, about three beats each, a few px apart; slabs like ledges: stood on, passed through from below) drifting up and down up to `FLOAT_RANGE` (18 px) with a slow swell of their own and the melody's brightness. Each settles to its own height over a beat while the player steps onto it or off it. Steps up toward the intensity's height are low (`CALM_STEP`). |
| Groove (a verse) | `contour` | Still ground whose height follows the intensity curve over a few bars: the stretch's plain ground steps toward it on a kick, up to 60% of the difficulty's `rise` up. |
| Chorus, and a drop after its set piece | `heave` | The ground is equaliser terrain: each run of plain ground becomes columns, one per step of the beat, that heave together with the bass on every beat (up on the beat, mostly down by the off-beat, up to 45% of `eqRange`, at most `HEAVE_MAX` 36 px; a gentle wave along the run, so neighbours are never more than a step apart). A run settles over half a beat as the player steps or lands onto it, unless it carries straight on from the stretch before's run; stepping off is always a step down. |
| Build | `climb` | Still ground climbing a step each stretch on a kick (half of `rise`, up to `CLIMB_TOP`), after the obstacles too unless the bar is a repeat; obstacles get denser (above). |
| Drop | `drop` | The set piece: three bars (four on fast songs), a staircase of up to seven steps (as many as fit under `MAX_TOP`), an arc of five gems over the plateau. |

Every style also steps down toward the intensity's height by up to a whole `rise` (no jump needed), so the ground comes back down after a drop.

**Changing style.** A new style starts on the downbeat of its first stretch, in the player's view: still ground ahead that the player reaches once it has reshaped, and that is in view on the downbeat, rests at the height the last stretch ended at and rises or falls into its own over `MORPH_BEATS` (two beats); floating slabs and heaving columns rest until the downbeat and start moving over `RAMP_BEATS` (two beats), every stretch of the style timed from that same downbeat.

**Repairs.** A stretch made easier moves less (its motion times the repair's `ease`); the last resort, flat ground, doesn't move at all.

**Stretches.** A moment belongs to the bar its beat is in (a low ceiling or a fill to the bar its approach starts in). A stretch is a bar (two on fast songs, whose bars are under 340 px; a drop's two or four); when an obstacle runs past its last bar it takes the next bar in, a few at most. Each stretch is laid out with the period of the tempo segment it starts in, on the grid's own beats, and on every other beat when beats are under 70 px.

**Teaching.** Dash and slam are taught on the first moment that needs them: dash on the first accent, or held sound or stop too long for a jump; slam on the first heavy kick, or (on normal and hard) fill, both ending in slam plates, which nothing but a slam gets through. The lesson is built at full size first, so it really needs its ability; around it goes everything that needs nothing new, so a lesson never changes a riff's repeat. Before an ability is taught nothing needs it: accents get no gate, held sounds and stops a pit a jump clears, heavy kicks nothing, fills no slam plates. The level keeps the lessons (`PlatformerLevel.lessons`), and the HUD shows a one-line hint with the current bindings ("Right click / Shift: dash") from a bar before the lesson until the player is past it, once per session.

**Reasons.** Every obstacle is kept with its moment and its reason (`PlatformerLevel.obstacles`: "Dash pit: held chord, 1.75 beats"), which the song details view shows.

The obstacle builders take their moments from outside (`pitAt(x, takeoff, air)`, `gateAt(x, time, top)`, `spikesAt(x, time, length, top)`, `overhangAt(x, from, to, h, clearance)`). Every obstacle is built so the press its moment asks for is made **on** the moment, as a musician would play it, never before: a dash gate is met `GATE_GRACE` after its beat, spikes and the drop's risers start where a jump pressed on the hit, or up to `PRESS_GRACE` (the same 0.1 s) after it, is already high enough (`clearFrom`, from `riseTime` of the jump), and a kick's pit starts under the player's middle. A recorded play of a punk song ("Save run data") showed why: with spikes the player had to be over on the hit and steps whose risers met the player on the beat, jumps had to come 150 ms to half a beat early and felt random.

## Difficulty

`DIFFICULTY` in `generate.ts`: easy, normal and hard change the sizes and how many of the common moments become obstacles, never which moment an obstacle belongs to: the share of kicks, snares and syncopations used (`hazard`), gap air times (a share of a full jump's), how far a held sound's or a stop's pit may reach between a jump's and a jump and dash's (`held`), how long low ceilings are and how much room they leave, height changes, spike lengths, how long bridges stay, how far equalisers move, step heights, how many hats get a coin, the floor of obstacles a busy bar and a chorus or drop bar has (`busyFloor`, `peakFloor`), whether fills end in slam plates (`slamFills`, normal and hard), whether sixteenths get obstacles (`sixteenths`, hard only), and on hard a variation on bars played a third time and a slam plate opening each phrase of a chorus or a drop (`phraseSlams`). The player picks it on the title screen (kept in the settings, `getDifficulty`); the HUD and the results say which.

Tuned on two synthetic songs in `src/core/analysis/test-songs.ts` (a 170 BPM rock song with a half-time breakdown, a quiet bridge at 150 and a last chorus at 180; a steady 128 BPM electronic track), whose tests (`level/test-songs.test.ts`) keep it so: real obstacles a minute go up from easy to hard (rock about 26, 33 and 49; electronic about 14, 17 and 29), and no difficulty can be finished without the dash or the slam. Easy takes 0.3 of the common moments, normal 0.7, hard 0.9.

## Always completable

`physics.ts` has the player's constants (jump velocity, gravity, coyote time, dash time, slam speed, box size) and `stepPlayer`, the one step function the game and the validator both run, at fixed `PHYSICS_DT` steps, against the level's moving parts at song time (`LevelWorld`). Its input is `{ jump, release, dash, slam }`; x is set from `world.xAt(t)` every step, never from input, so a scroll that speeds up and slows down with the tempo map needs nothing more from the physics or the validator.

The validator (`validate.ts`) is a search over the player's inputs. With x fixed by time, every candidate at a moment is at the same x, so a candidate is only its height, speed, ground, dash charge and timer and slam. Every three steps each candidate tries doing nothing, jumping, letting go of jump (a lower jump, so the game's variable height is in the search), dashing and slamming, whichever can change something. Candidates in the same state are merged, and the rest thinned evenly by height within each kind (on the ground, dash ready, dashing, dash spent, slamming), so those with a dash still to use aren't crowded out. Feet more than a step below the lowest surface (`LOWEST_TOP`) can never land again and are dropped. It can only miss ways through, never invent one, so a level it passes can really be finished. `validateLevel(level, without)` can leave dash or slam out, so tests prove a lesson needs its ability.

The generator builds a bar (or two) at a time and runs the search through it before going on. Each stretch's search stops on a decision step while the player's box is still wholly inside the stretch, so every candidate has only met what is built, just as in the finished level, and the incremental proof agrees with `validateLevel`. A stretch nobody gets through, or that leaves nobody on the ground, is built again easier, every obstacle shrunk on its own moment, never moved to another beat: narrower pits, lower steps, longer bridges and shorter spikes (`ease` 0.7, then 0.45), then a ledge in each pit, then only its most salient obstacle, and at last plain ground at the height the last stretch ended, which is always passable. `Chunk.repairs` counts the tries; the song details view shows them as "eased". A three-minute song builds in about a second.

Deterministic: the generator's randomness is seeded by the song's map seed, the difficulty and the bar, so the same song at the same difficulty always gives the same level, and a repair never changes the bars after it.

## Playing it

`src/games/platformer/game.ts` starts a session: it builds the level in the worker (at the difficulty picked on the title screen), decodes the song and runs Phaser.

- **Song time only.** Every frame the session reads `AudioClock.time`, adds the display lead (`DisplayLead`: the frame is seen a frame or two after it is drawn, see `docs/core.md`, Timing and latency) and `PlatformerRun.update` (`run.ts`) runs the fixed `PHYSICS_DT` steps of `stepPlayer` up to it, so the game moves exactly as the validator checked. The scene (`scene.ts`) draws the level as it is at that time: equaliser heights, bridges, spikes, gates, the camera at `cameraAt(level, t)` (so the cube stays at the anchor; through a count-in it holds at the time the run resumes from, where the cube waits), the squash, the effects. Nothing moves by frame time.
- **Feel.** Coyote time is in the physics. The jump buffer (`JUMP_BUFFER`) is in the run: a jump or dash press counts for that long, so one pressed just before landing happens on landing. A slam press only counts in its own frame (held over, it would cut the next jump short). Variable height is `release` in `stepPlayer`: letting go of jump while rising cuts the speed to `JUMP_CUT_VELOCITY`; the validator lets go too, so short hops are part of what it proves.
- **Controls** (`controls.ts`, from the bindings in Settings): by default Space, W or ↑ to jump; Shift, J, X or right click to dash; S, ↓ or left click to slam; E or L for overdrive; Esc to pause. Gamepad: A to jump, X or RB to dash, B or down (d-pad or left stick) to slam, Y for overdrive, Start to pause. Mouse buttons play on the game's surface (`mousedown` per button, so both at once do both), never on HUD or menu buttons, and the browser's context menu is kept shut there. Every device's presses go the same way into the run's fixed steps. The pause menu lists the current bindings.
- **Deaths.** The run goes back to its checkpoint: the start of the stretch the current song section begins in (stretches start on a bar line on plain ground). The death effect plays for `DEATH_TIME` of song time, then the music rewinds to one bar before the checkpoint and the player waits through that bar as a 4-3-2-1 count-in. At the start the count-in is a bar of silence: `AudioClock.play` from a negative time.
- **Score** (`run.ts`; see [Scoring](#scoring)): judged actions, a combo, overdrive, streaks, clean sections and boosters.
- **Look** (`scene.ts`, Neon arcade after `docs/concept/look.html`): bloom on the camera (Phaser's `AddEffectBloom`) over the synth grid rolling toward the player, faint wave lines and the violet speaker sending out a ring on every kick; equalisers and ground with bright caps, floating slabs fading downward with a faint line under them, dotted cyan bridges (a faint dotted outline where a bridge will be, blinking in over the half beat before its snare), orange spikes that rise a little just before they come out; orange dash gates humming on the beat, whose bars fly on ahead when dashed through; overhangs in the world's violet hanging from the sky, with an orange face where they would hit; slam plates as violet slabs with bright cracks down from their top, drawn every frame so one the player slammed through is gone (`PlatformerRun.brokenAt`; whole again after a death before it), bursting into violet shards. The cube flips once per jump (from `run.jumpedAt`), squashes and stretches and leaves a light trail; a dash stretches it long and flat with cyan afterimages and speed lines streaming back, so it reads as a burst forward though the cube stays at the anchor; a slam stretches it tall with a streak above, and its landing sends a shockwave along the ground, magenta on the beat and cyan off it. Landings raise dust, pickups spark, deaths shatter the cube and shake the screen. Every effect is drawn from song time, so a pause freezes it and a rewind plays it again.
- **The look follows the mood** (`LevelMap.mood`, read per frame at the song time): as the intensity rises the grid rolls faster (half a row a beat when calm to one and a half when loud, its roll the running sum per beat so it never jumps), its lines get thicker and brighter and turn toward magenta, the bloom's strength rises (1.1 to 2), the wave lines grow taller and thicker and the magenta one comes up, the horizon glows more and the speaker's thump and rings grow (from about a third to more than full). Calm parts are dim and violet-led; loud ones bright with more magenta. The colours keep their meanings: the world stays violet, magenta only as the beat's accent.
- **Beat marks**: a magenta notch under the ground's cap where the cube's middle is on each beat, longer on a bar's first, flashing as the cube crosses it (`#drawBeats`, drawn every frame at the ground's height then; no flash with effects reduced). The ground is a ruler of the pulse: what sits on a mark is pressed on that beat, so the rhythm ahead can be read and played by ear.
- **Kick and snare on the platforms**: every platform in view pulses on the kick (its cap glows and a hot line squashes into it, for a third of a beat, stronger on louder kicks and louder music), and its free edges flash magenta on the snare (0.14 s). Drawn only, over the tiles: collisions stay exact.
- **The drop's set piece**: through each drop chunk the sky flashes magenta and violet on every beat (brighter than before), two big waveforms ripple (taller), bigger particle bursts fly from the player on every beat over the magenta staircase and DROP thumps at the top; then it calms down over two bars.
- **Effects intensity** (Settings): reduced turns off the flashes (the drop glows steadily instead, the snare doesn't flash the platforms, the kick's pulse is softer and doesn't squash) and the screen shake; it follows `prefers-reduced-motion` until the player picks.
- **Frame budget** (`scene.ts`, `perf.ts`): still ground, steps and ledges never move, so they are drawn once into 512 px tile textures as they scroll in (`#bakeTile`); only what moves is drawn every frame (solids with keys: equalisers, heaving columns, floating slabs, ground reshaping at a change of style), heaving columns with square caps (rounded ones cost twice the draw commands); the kick and snare pulse is a few rects per platform in view; bridges are pooled images of one dot texture; equalisers, spikes and pickups are culled to the view by binary search; the frame loop allocates nothing (the trail is a ring of typed arrays, floating texts and the frame state are reused). The bloom's threshold pass writes to a half-size target, so the blur works on a quarter of the pixels and the blend scales it back up. `FrameMonitor` times every frame (from Phaser's `PRE_STEP` to `POST_RENDER`): when most frames of a second are over 19 ms apart, the quality steps down (`QUALITY`: fewer blur passes, then a quarter-size bloom, fewer particles, fewer wave points), and Settings says "Lowered automatically to keep the game smooth" next to Effects while the game runs. Measured at 1920×1080 on a 150 BPM rock test song (desktop, RTX 5070 Ti, Chromium; frames stepped at 60 fps of song time): CPU per frame calm 3.4 → 1.7 ms, busy stretches with bridges 5.0 → 2.2 ms mean and 9.4 → 4.6 ms worst, drops 2.8 → 2.5 ms mean, worst under 5 ms; GPU per frame 0.47 → 0.26 ms, the bloom's share 0.41 → about 0.18 ms. With the terrain styles (Nov 2026, in Jig's headless Chromium at 1920×1080, CPU per frame stepped at 60 fps of song time over three seconds, on a synthetic rock song with tempo and mood changes): calm floating slabs 1.8 ms mean, 3.4 ms worst; the drop 2.4 ms mean, 6 ms worst; a heaving chorus 2.8 ms mean, 3.5 ms at the 95th percentile, 6.8 ms worst (4.1 ms mean before the square caps). The playtest pass (Nov 2026, the rock test song on hard with autoplay, frames stepped at 60 fps of song time, full quality held, at 1920×1080; the machine was about 2.5 times slower than for the numbers above, a calm stretch 4.5 ms mean): the drops 6.7–6.8 ms mean, 9 ms at the 95th percentile, 16–17 ms worst; a heaving chorus 4.6 ms mean, 7.1 ms worst; overdrive in a chorus 4.9 ms mean, 7.7 ms worst. The first frame of the first drop took 13 to 48 ms in a fresh session, the DROP callout's text going to the GPU: it is drawn all but invisibly for the first frames now (`#warmFrames`). In headless Chromium the frames come about 60 ms apart whatever they cost, which the monitor reads as slow and lowers the quality within seconds: hold it at full (patch `scene.monitor.frame` to pass a 16.7 ms interval) when measuring. A laptop is several times slower on both, so the numbers are for comparing changes, not a promise.
- **Settings** (`src/shell/settings.ts`, from the title screen and the pause menu): music volume (a shared gain node, applied at once), effects intensity, the frame rate readout (Show puts FPS and the mean CPU ms per frame in the HUD's bottom right corner), and the audio latency offset, nudged in 5 ms steps or calibrated by tapping along to a 100 BPM metronome (the median lateness of the taps; uneven taps are turned down), and the controls (`src/shell/controls-settings.ts`): a game picker (opening on the game being played or picked), then that game's actions (`GameDefinition.controls`; the platformer's jump, dash, slam and overdrive) in aligned columns, three key slots, two mouse and two gamepad slots each. Click a slot and press the key or button (a mouse button anywhere, a newly pressed gamepad button); Escape cancels, Backspace clears. An input another action had moves over and the row says so ("Shift was dash; now slam"); bindings are shared by every game, and only actions no game uses together (the platformer's slam, the tunnel's left) may share an input (`rivalActions`). Esc and Start always pause and can't be bound. Reset puts one action, or all, back to the defaults. The bindings are kept in the stored settings (`getBindings` in `src/core/settings.ts`); an action added after bindings were saved (overdrive, the tunnel's left and right) gets its defaults, less any input the player already gave another action.
- **Run recordings** (`recording.ts`): "Save run data" in the pause menu and on the results screen downloads the session as JSON (`waveland-run-<song>-<date>.json`), to compare a real play with the music afterwards. It holds the song's level map (beats, segments, hits, sections) and the level (obstacles with their moments, solids, scroll), the setup (the latency offset, the `outputLatency` and `baseLatency` the browser reports, the frame interval and display lead, the screen), and every attempt (a retry starts a new one): each press with the song time heard when it was made (`heard`, from the key or mouse event's own timestamp; null for a gamepad, which has no events), the time heard at the frame that read it (`frame`) and the time the run acted on it (`run`, plus the display lead), jump releases, the run's takeoffs, landings, dashes and slams at their exact step times, the judgements, deaths and pickups, where the music played from (count-ins, respawns, resumes) and the results.
- **HUD and menus** are HTML over the canvas, the shared ones (`src/games/shared/`: HUD, pause menu, results screen, number formats) with the platformer's own pieces on top (`ui.ts`: booster slots, first-time hints, its results breakdown and rank rules): song and BPM (the tempo of the moment), beat dots, score and the combo meter, the overdrive meter with its bound key and the three booster slots (bottom left, in fixed places), the count-in, progress with the drops marked, the first-time hint for an ability; the pause menu (Esc: Resume, Restart, Settings, Quit, and the controls as bound) and the results screen (score and rank, the breakdown in aligned columns, the rank thresholds; Retry, Back to menu). Switching away from the tab pauses. Resume holds the player where they stopped and rewinds the music `RESUME_BEATS` (two beats) for a short count-in, so play never starts again mid-jump without warning.

## Scoring

All in `PlatformerRun` (`run.ts`), on song time; the scene and HUD only draw what it says.

- **Judged actions.** Every jump takeoff, jump landing, dash and slam landing is judged against the nearest beat *or eighth* of the grid (`judgeAt`, `offGrid`): Perfect within `PERFECT_WINDOW` (50 ms), Good within `GOOD_WINDOW` (100 ms), else Miss. On fast songs the windows shrink to a share of the eighth (`PERFECT_SHARE` 18%, `GOOD_SHARE` 36%), so not everything lands on the grid. A jump's landing is only judged when it is on the grid: where it lands is the takeoff's and the ground's doing, and a miss there would break a combo the player timed right (playing the test songs, a third of the landings after a perfect takeoff fell off the grid). Song time is `AudioClock.time`, which already takes off the output latency and the player's latency offset from Settings, so an action is judged against the beat as it is heard; a takeoff or a dash the player pressed is judged `inputLead` (the display lead) before the step it acts on, the song time it was pressed at. Points: `POINTS.perfect` 300, `POINTS.good` 100. An **accent** is worth `ACCENT_FACTOR` (×2): a dash within `GOOD_WINDOW` of a snare, or a slam landing within it of a kick or a downbeat (kicks and snares of at least 0.3 intensity from the level map's events, `musicOf`). The scene floats a small word at the cube: PERFECT in magenta, GOOD in ink, MISS muted, ACCENT in magenta.
- **Combo.** Judged actions that aren't misses and every pickup add one; a miss or a hit (a death, or a shield taking one) breaks it. The multiplier is 1 + combo / `COMBO_STEP` (8), up to `MAX_MULTIPLIER` (×4); the HUD's combo meter under the score shows the multiplier, the steps to the next one and the count.
- **Overdrive.** A meter filled by perfects (`METER_PERFECT`, a twelfth) and gems (`METER_GEM`, a quarter), not while overdrive runs. Full, the overdrive button (bound in Settings like the others: E or L, gamepad Y, no mouse button by default) starts it: `OVERDRIVE_BEATS` (16, 4 bars on the tempo map) of score ×`OVERDRIVE_FACTOR` (2) on top of the combo. Started within `GOOD_WINDOW` of a downbeat it earns `DOWNBEAT_BONUS` (1000) times the multiplier. The HUD's meter (bottom left) says what fills it, "Ready · press E" with the bound key, or "Score ×2" while it runs down. The cube burns magenta with flames, the trail turns magenta, the grid leans magenta, the bloom rises and the sky flares magenta on every beat (a steady glow with effects reduced). It changes nothing in the physics, so it is never needed to finish.
- **Streaks.** Every `STREAK_PERFECTS` (10) perfects in a row (a good or a miss starts over) burst for `STREAK_BONUS` (1000) times the multiplier and overdrive.
- **Clean sections.** Passing a checkpoint (a section's start) with no hit since the last one, or since coming back to it, earns `CLEAN_BONUS` (2000), shown as a banner at the section change; the song's end closes the last section.
- **Boosters.** Pickups the generator places (`boosters` in `generate.ts`): one in a section's first bars and one more for every `BOOSTER_BARS` (8) bars it goes on, from the second section on, never in a drop's set piece or around a lesson, in turn shield, magnet, extra dash, coin rush (from a seeded start). Each sits `BOOSTER_ABOVE` over the head at the top of a full jump taken on a beat, simulated with `stepPlayer`: the jump and a moment after it must be safe; over a pit when the section's first bars have one (risky), else over plain ground (optional); a short hop passes under. The validator never sees pickups and no obstacle needs one, so levels stay completable without them. Picked up, a booster scores `POINTS.booster` and chains the combo.
  - **Shield** (cyan ring around the cube): the next hit (wall, gate, spikes) or pit fall is survived. The combo breaks and the player bounces in an arc, blinking and ignoring collisions (`PlatformerRun.rescue`), onto the first surface from `RESCUE_MIN` to `RESCUE_MAX` after the hit where standing still for `SAFE_TIME` is safe (checked with `stepPlayer`; not under a ceiling). None within reach: the hit is a death after all.
  - **Magnet** (magenta field): for `MAGNET_BEATS` (4 bars) coins within `MAGNET_RADIUS` (110 px) of the box are collected and fly to the cube.
  - **Extra dash** (two chevrons behind the cube): a second dash in the air once the first is over, until the next landing.
  - **Coin rush** (four small coins circling the cube): for `RUSH_BEATS` (4 bars) a coin scores `RUSH_FACTOR` (5) times as much and fills the overdrive meter by `METER_RUSH_COIN` (1/32), so the hats' coins are worth chasing; with a magnet running too they pour in.
  The HUD has a fixed slot for each with its icon, lit while it is on, with a bar for the time left (full until a shield or extra dash is used).
- **Checkpoints.** A death puts the score, combo, perfect streak, overdrive meter and timer, clean sections, collected pickups and boosters back to what they were at the checkpoint; the best combo reached stays.
- **Results.** Perfect, good and miss counts and overdrive uses are over the whole play, rewinds included; clean sections count against the number of checkpoints. **Timing** is (perfects + half the goods) / judged actions. **Rank** (`RANKS`): S at 85% timing and no deaths, A at 70% and at most 2 deaths, B at 50%, C for finishing; the results screen spells out the thresholds.

## Where to look

- Profiling and playtesting: in dev (`npm run dev`) the running session is `window.waveland` (its `scene`, `run`, `clock`), for timing frames from the browser's console. `waveland.autoplay()` lets the level play itself: `solveLevel` (`validate.ts`) keeps how each of the search's candidates got where it is and returns the presses of one way through, and the run plays them step by step (`PlatformerRun.autoplay`), so a whole song can be watched and timed at any difficulty. The test songs load from the console too: `const m = await import('/src/core/analysis/test-songs.ts')`, then drop `m.wavFile(...)` of `m.rockSong(44100)` or `m.electroSong(44100)` on the page.
- Song details view, below the analysis: a side-on preview of the level at a picked difficulty, following the playhead (`src/shell/level-preview.ts`). Each obstacle in the camera's view gets a numbered mark; pointing at one (or tapping it) says what it is and why, and the list under the canvas ("In view, and why") gives the same for every numbered obstacle, since hover isn't enough. The timelines above show the moments the level is built from (a MOMENTS lane and strip, and a REPEATS strip lettering the bars the song plays again).
- `generateLevelInWorker(map, difficulty)` builds it off the main thread; a three-minute song takes about a second.
